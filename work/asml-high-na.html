EUV source for High NA

Mechanical design on the light source of ASML’s first High NA lithography systems, the machines built to print the next generations of chips.

Context

Every chip generation needs smaller features, and the light that prints them sets the limit.

ASML’s EUV systems print with 13.5 nm light. High NA raises the numerical aperture of the optics from 0.33 to 0.55, using larger, anamorphic mirrors, to reach a resolution of 8 nm. The TWINSCAN EXE:5000 is the first High NA system; ASML shipped its first modules to Intel in December 2023. The EXE:5200B is its successor for volume production, and ASML credits an improved EUV light source for its higher productivity.

My role

I joined ASML as a mechanical design engineer on the EUV source for High NA, working on the EXE:5000 and then the EXE:5200. The source is where the light starts: tin droplets hit by laser pulses become a plasma that emits 13.5 nm light, which a collector mirror gathers and hands to the scanner.

I now work on next-generation platform development.

The details of the work are confidential, so this sheet shows the system and the public context rather than my designs.

The system

0.55NA

Numerical aperture of the High NA optics, up from 0.33 in NXE systems.

8nm

Resolution, with 40% more imaging contrast than NXE systems.

2.9×

Higher transistor density in a single exposure on the EXE:5200B, compared with NXE systems.
